# Investment Lab Instructions For Gray

## Working Style

- Explain changes clearly and simply.
- Prefer hands-on learning over long theory.
- When making code changes, explain the important files and concepts afterwards.
- Do not hide complexity; introduce it gradually.
- Keep the first version simple and working before making it clever.

## Project

This project is Gray's Investment Lab: a learning project for coding, AI tools, and investment thinking.

The goal is not automated trading. The goal is to build tools that help with research, tracking, journalling, and learning.

Useful project outcomes may include:

- research notes and company summaries
- watchlists and trackers
- learning notebooks or scripts
- simple dashboards
- journalling tools
- checklists for repeatable research

## Wiki Linking Rules

- Treat the vault as a linked wiki, not a pile of separate notes.
- Use Obsidian wiki links in the format `[[Note Name]]` when referring to projects, people, topics, tools, companies, and recurring ideas.
- Prefer creating reusable notes for important concepts, projects, decisions, and learning topics.
- When adding a session note, link it to the relevant project, area, person, company, or learning note.
- Do not over-link every word; link things Gray is likely to revisit.

## Notes To Keep

For each coding session, maintain a note like:

`Journal/YYYY-MM-DD.md`

Include:

- what we built
- what broke
- what Gray learned
- questions to revisit
- next steps

For investment research, use notes like:

`Learning/Investing/Companies/{Company Name}.md`

Include:

- what the company does
- why it might be interesting
- risks
- valuation thoughts
- sources
- decision or no decision

## Safety

- Do not include passwords, API keys, tokens, or private financial account details in the wiki.
- Do not connect to real brokerage accounts.
- Do not give financial advice as certainty.
- Treat investment output as learning and research, not instructions to buy or sell.
- If real money or personal financial decisions are involved, slow down and clearly separate research from advice.
